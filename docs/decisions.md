# Decision Log

Analytic and engineering choices, newest first. One short entry each: what, why, alternatives rejected.

## 2026-10-25 — Household size by municipality and the bedroom pairing
- **What:** `ine_households_size` (census 2011 and 2021, 1, 2, 3 and 4 or more persons) from the household tables already used for the totals, with the size dimension left open (`INE_HOUSEHOLD_SIZE`); the census-stock builder now takes only the total rows (`_only_totals`). `analysis/housing_mix.py` pairs bedrooms with persons (T0-T1 with 1 ... T4+ with 4+).
- **Why:** RQ2.6 needs demand by type; household size is the one dimension the data give at municipal level. The pairing is the simplest transparent mapping and is labelled nominal everywhere.
- **Reading:** a gap between the mixes is not a count of homes missing (a couple can live in a T3), and a negative association with prices is read as a regional pattern (cheap, ageing interior), not as a cause.
- **Rejected:** a conversion table of persons per bedroom without a source; weighting municipalities (the gap is a median across municipalities, not a national average; the national table gives that).
- **Lead:** single-person households are now observable by municipality, which `p2_11` (RQ3.5) listed as not observable.

## 2026-10-25 — Legal floor for the IMI rate and what it means for the simulator's cut
- **What:** `config.IMI_URBAN_MIN_PCT` (0.30) and `revenue.imi_headroom` / `capped_cut` measure how far councils can cut inside the law; `p3_05` reports that headroom as 0.027 pp (8.2% of the bill) in 2025, 199 of 300 councils being on the floor. `revenue.dwelling_sales_value` is the one formula for the value of dwellings sold, now also used by the engine (same number).
- **Why:** the 0.05 pp IMI package of the simulator is 15% of the bill. Data show every rate in 2012-2025 between 0.30% and 0.45%, so a uniform 0.05 pp cut is not available in full to 233 of the 300 councils.
- **Kept:** the package and its costs (the lever is a policy option that would need a lower floor); the comment in `scenarios.yaml` and the p3 report say so. Rejected: silently capping the lever (it would change published results), and using revenue per council (no municipal revenue exists, so incidence by municipality is not estimated).
- **Reading:** IMI and IMT are municipal revenue, so "central revenue" is unaffected; the loss is the councils' (the national accounts also hold AIMI, which is the State's).

## 2026-10-25 — National IMI and IMT revenue from the national accounts
- **What:** `eurostat_property_tax` takes D29A and D214C of general government from Eurostat (`gov_10a_taxag`) with nominal GDP; `analysis/revenue.py` and `p3_04` read them against house prices and the weighted average municipal IMI rate (fixed 2021 dwelling weights, so only rate changes move it).
- **Why:** no municipal revenue is machine-readable. The national accounts are a different measure from the simulator's anchors (accrual, not assessed or cash) but agree within 3% for 2024, which supports using the anchors for scale.
- **Reading:** the IMI base is revenue over the average rate, so it also carries reassessments, exemptions and AIMI; the gap to the nominal price is how far assessed values lag the market, not a measured assessment ratio. IMT is a product of price and sales, which are not separated.
- **Rejected:** the PC_GDP unit (rounded to one decimal); replacing the simulator anchors (they are what a 2024 cut would apply to); reading the 2025 value as final.

## 2026-10-25 — Nationality groups in the foreign-resident regression
- **What:** `p2_14` splits the foreign-resident change of `p2_02` into six groups (`demography.NATIONALITY_GROUPS`: Brazil, Lusophone Africa, Eastern Europe, China, UK, all other) with the same controls and effects, and tests equality of the six coefficients (`panel.equal_coefficients`, Wald).
- **Why these groups:** each is large enough to move a municipality's population in some places (the smallest, China and the UK, are 2.7% and 4.5% of the 2023 total) and each follows its own trend (Eastern Europe fell by a third while Brazil quadrupled); single countries would give 11 mostly empty terms.
- **Reading:** the equality test is the claim (the groups differ with year effects, much less within a region); no group is called a cause of price rises.
- **Rejected:** shares of each nationality in the population (levels do not vary enough within a municipality); regressing on all 11 codes; grouping by income or legal route (not observed).

## 2026-10-25 — Fundamentals as a constant-burden benchmark, read through changes in the gap
- **What:** `p2_13` (`macro.financeable_price`, `price_change_parts`) sets implied price = average wage / payment per euro borrowed (30-year annuity at the agreed rate), scaled to equal the actual price on average 2003-2025, and splits each episode's price change into wages, the rate and the gap.
- **Why:** a regression of price on wages and rates over 23 years and two regimes would hand back a coefficient with no way to tell "unexplained" from misspecified; the accounting split is exact and shows where the benchmark stops working (2021-2025), and the result holds under APRC, 20/40-year terms and other windows (gap +40 to +60).
- **Rejected:** a cointegration or error-correction model (n = 23); reading the gap as a bubble or as expectations (it also holds rationing, rich or cash buyers, supply); a real-terms version (nominal wage and payment are what a buyer faces, the deflator cancels).

## 2026-10-25 — Household debt from BPstat, tested like the rate: prices lead credit
- **What:** new dataset `bp_household_debt` (six BPstat series, domain 18: housing-loan stock in euros and % of GDP, its transactions-based growth, household debt in euros, % of GDP and % of disposable income), kept at quarter-end months. `p2_12` (`analysis/credit.py`) compares the real housing-loan stock with real prices by episode and runs the `p2_08` timing test with credit growth as the regressor (`macro.rate_timing(x_col=...)`), plus a placebo with next year's credit growth.
- **Why the stock and its growth, not new lending:** the new-loan volume in `bp_mortgage` covers loans fixed up to a year only and falls when borrowers move to fixed rates; the stock covers every lender and the growth rate is transactions-based, so write-offs and loan sales do not count as repayment.
- **Why a lead-lag test and not a regression of price on debt:** the stock and the price are both trending in opposite directions over 2013-2024, so any levels regression would find a spurious negative; the lagged changes with a placebo show the order (prices first).
- **Rejected:** debt to GDP as a regressor (the denominator is also driven by the cycle); a VAR (74 quarters, two regimes); loans per unit of price as leverage (it ignores the housing stock and the share bought without a loan).

## 2026-10-25 — Rents: forecast the slow index and price-to-rent, keep rents out of the simulator
- **What:** `p4_05` applies the `p4_01` methods to annual series (ETS without seasonal forms, `ANNUAL_METHODS`): the OECD real rent index and price-to-rent, rolling origin from 2007, method chosen per series.
- **Why per series:** the pooled rule of `p4_01` would have picked ARIMA for both, though it is 26% worse than no change for rents; a model must beat the baseline on the series it is used for.
- **Why not in the simulator:** the index covers all leases (+0.2% real 2020-2024 against +24.0% for new leases), the new-lease series is five years long, and a forecast of the first says little about a tenant's affordability.
- **Rejected:** forecasting INE's municipal rents (five annual points); using the OECD rent series back to 1959 (hyperinflation and rent regimes of other eras).

## 2026-10-24 — Age structure as a cross-section next to the foreign-resident change
- **What:** `p2_11` (`analysis/age_structure.py`) takes five age bands from the 2011-2023 five-year groups and regresses the log change of price-to-income 2016-2023 on the change in the 25-44 and 65+ shares, within district, with coast and density class, and with the foreign-resident change.
- **Why:** the 65+ share is already in the P2.02 panel; the new question is the household-forming ages, and young adults arrive as migrants (correlation 0.58 with foreign-resident change), so an age coefficient without that control would restate the migration result. Rule for reading it fixed first.
- **Rejected:** a panel with the 25-44 share and municipality effects (the share moves with migrants and the cycle, and ten years of data add little); an "ageing-in-place" split (residents only, no cohort flows).

## 2026-10-24 — Validation schemes tested with a plain regression, scored on common targets
- **What:** `p4_04` (`forecasting.validation_schemes`, `scheme_scores`) scores one least-squares model of the h-quarter change on four lagged changes under rolling origin, random folds, purged leave-one-out and purged blocks, all on the targets the rolling origin can score.
- **Why:** M5 asks how much random splits mislead; a plain model isolates the scheme from model choice, and common targets stop a different sample from reading as a different skill. The reading rule (gap beyond repeat spread) was fixed first.
- **Rejected:** a tree ensemble or other ML as the test model (would add complexity for its own sake, against the project guardrail); re-scoring ETS/ARIMA with random folds (they are refitted sequentially, random folds do not apply).

## 2026-10-24 — Centres by size, distance by border steps
- **What:** `p2_10` (`analysis/centres.py`) calls a municipality a centre if it had at least 100,000 residents in 2021 (25 municipalities), measures distance as border steps to the nearest centre on the queen contiguity graph (the one used for Moran's I, no links across the sea), compares medians by distance, then repeats it with NUTS 3 fixed effects (errors clustered by region) and compares Lisboa and Porto with the rest of their metropolitan areas.
- **Why:** it answers RQ4.3 without a functional urban area, which the data do not contain (INE's parish typology is not ingested), and the same weights as `p2_01` keep neighbours consistent. The size rule is transparent and does not depend on the outcome.
- **Rejected:** only Lisboa and Porto as centres (two points, no gradient); straight-line distance in km (islands, and the river Tejo makes neighbours across the estuary far in steps but near in km); choosing the cut-off by the result.

## 2026-10-24 — Kinds of price: balanced municipalities, appraisal as the only second price measure
- **What:** `p1_08` (`analysis/price_types.py`) compares new and existing homes (2019-2025), appraised flats and villas (2011-2025) and appraisal against transaction price (2016-2025). Trends use municipalities observed in every compared year ("balanced"); the unbalanced median is shown beside the appraisal ratio; real values use the project deflator.
- **Why:** appraisals are suppressed for small municipalities and coverage grows from 198 to 261 municipalities, which moved the unbalanced ratio the opposite way (1.04 to 1.03) from the balanced one (1.04 to 0.94). Flats against villas uses a within-municipality ratio for the same reason.
- **Limits that shape the claim:** no price by number of rooms and no asking prices exist in the data, so RQ1.2 and RQ1.8 stay "partly"; appraisal against transaction is a gap between two measures with different timing, not asking against paid.
- **Rejected:** treating the bank appraisal as an asking price; a regression of the gap on municipality traits (not enough to separate coverage from behaviour).

## 2026-10-24 — National accounts for wages: Eurostat series, labour force survey unemployment
- **What:** `eurostat_macro` combines Eurostat GDP growth (`nama_10_gdp`, chain-linked volumes), the unemployment rate from `lfsa_urgan` (1995 onward) and compensation per employee (compensation of employees `nama_10_a10` over employees `nama_10_a10_e`, whole economy). `p2_09` uses them against national real and nominal prices, annual 1995-2025.
- **Why:** it gives a wage concept thirty years long; INE's income per taxable person starts in 2015 and the OECD's income per head is not a wage. The shorter `une_rt_a` is skipped because it starts in 2009 for Portugal.
- **Design:** yearly real price growth on wage growth, change in unemployment and GDP growth, Newey-West errors (2 lags), same year and one year earlier, with last year's growth, without 2020-2022, and in nominal terms (so the deflator shared by real prices and real wages cannot drive the result). No fixed-effects or VAR: 30 observations.
- **Rejected:** claiming that wages or unemployment cause prices (cycle co-movement; credit and expectations omitted); using GDP growth as the headline driver (it adds nothing in the same year once the labour market is in, and is slightly negative when lagged).

## 2026-10-24 — Rates and prices: pre-specified timing test, episodes from turning points
- **What:** `p2_08` tests whether the rate moves prices with one specification fixed before the lag profile was drawn: a year's real price growth on the rate change of the year before (non-overlapping windows), Newey-West errors (6 lags), then last year's growth, a next-year-rate placebo, nominal prices and the two halves of the sample. Episodes are bounded by the 2008Q3 crisis start and by turning points computed from the data (price low, rate low, rate peak).
- **Why:** with one national series and a persistent rate, choosing the lag with the best correlation would manufacture a result (the profile peaks at 1 quarter, where the specification we fixed is 4). The headline is the pre-specified one; the profile is drawn only as description.
- **Rejected:** new loan volume as a credit-supply regressor (our series is loans fixed up to one year only, so it falls when borrowers move to fixed rates); a cointegration or VAR model (two regimes, 93 quarters: it would not beat the simple test); a causal reading of any coefficient.

## 2026-10-24 — Population split: national from 1960, municipal 2011-2023, revision kept apart
- **What:** `p1_07` splits population change into natural change and net migration with Eurostat `demo_gind` (national, 1960-2025) and INE's municipal demographic indicators 0008269-0008271 (2011-2023). INE's +201,000 post-census revision of 2020-2021 is a separate `revision_n` nationally, and the municipal windows are 2012-2019 and 2022-2023.
- **Why:** the municipal series has no pre-2011 data, so the thirty-year answer (RQ3.1) is national; the revision sits in the municipal net migration of 2020-2021 (250,000 in 2021 against 72,000 in Eurostat's flow), which would show a migration surge that is partly a statistical correction.
- **Rejected:** deriving natural change and migration from the age-sex population table (no births or deaths); using the crude rates (0008262-0008265: rounded, and the counts are available); dropping 2020-2021 from the national chart (the revision is shown instead, so the levels still reconcile).
- **Found by:** reading INE's indicator names one at a time around the id of the crude birth rate (0008264); a wide scan of ids with many parallel requests got this machine blocked by INE for a while, so look ids up singly and slowly.

## 2026-10-23 — Indicator definitions tested one at a time against the base (M1)
- **What:** `descriptive.compare_definitions` and `p1_06`. Six alternatives to price-to-income (fixed 100 m² dwelling, mean income, bank appraisals, existing dwellings, rent-to-income, mortgage burden), each compared with the base on the municipalities where both exist: rank correlation of the 2024 level and of the log change over a window, share with the same sign, share worsening, top-20 overlap.
- **Why one at a time and per-window:** the series differ in length (existing-dwelling prices from 2019, rents 2020-2024), so each alternative gets the longest window it supports and the base is recomputed on the same municipalities and window; a common window would drop the informative comparisons.
- **Trend panel:** the median of each municipality's own change since 2016, not the median of levels. The median of levels changes identity from year to year and moved the fixed-size variant's rise from 17% to 10% for no reason that concerns affordability.
- **Reading:** a high rank correlation says the choice does not matter for where, not for how much; both are reported. The sign of the change is not meaningful where the change is within about 3.5%.
- **Rejected:** a composite or "best" indicator (the definitions answer different questions: purchase, rent, financing), testing definitions jointly (the alternatives are one-step changes that can each be explained).

## 2026-10-23 — Foreign residents described from titles, with the 2023 step kept (RQ3.2)
- **What:** `analysis/demography.py` and `p1_05`: totals, nationalities, concentration, distribution by density, coast and district, and the location of each nationality, from `aima_foreigners` (2014-2023) and the panel's population.
- **Why the 2023 step is kept, not smoothed:** it is +34% in the median municipality and above +20% in 222 of 308, so it is a national movement of the series, not a local event; dropping or interpolating 2023 would invent a figure. It is stated in the title note of the chart and in the limitations.
- **Why titles are not called immigrants:** the series excludes naturalised citizens and the foreign-born, so the growth contribution (foreign titles over population change) is reported as a share of population change without splitting natural change or emigration, which the data do not provide.
- **Rejected:** a decomposition of population growth into natural change and net migration (no municipal natural change or migration series is ingested; RQ3.1 stays open), nationality-level price regressions (p2 uses the total).

## 2026-10-23 — Renting versus buying as a user cost with assumed components (RQ1.6)
- **What:** `features/rent_vs_buy.py` and `p1_04`. Yearly cost of owning (interest on the loan, return forgone on the deposit, IMI on part of the price, upkeep, buying costs over ten years) as a % of the price, against the rent yield; the difference is the break-even price growth. Municipal for 2020-2024 (the only years with rents), national for 2003-2025 with the OECD price-to-rent index scaled to the municipal yield. The columns are computed by `add_indicators`, not stored in the panel.
- **Why a user cost, not a cash comparison:** payments include principal, which is saving; the economic cost of owning is interest, forgone return, taxes and upkeep. The cash view is kept as a second indicator because it is what a household must pay each month.
- **Why assumed parameters:** the project has no data on deposit returns, taxable values or IMT paid, and inventing precision would mislead. They live in `config.py`, the sensitivity is a table, and the conclusion about change over time rests on the observed mortgage rate, not on them.
- **Anchoring:** the OECD rent index covers all leases (slow), municipal rents are new leases (market): they diverge by -0.5 to +0.6 pp in 2020-2024. The scale is calibrated on 2020-2024 as a geometric mean rather than on one year, and the gap is published.
- **Rejected:** adding the columns to the municipal panel (nothing else uses them yet, and the panel feeds the dashboard); modelling expected appreciation (the break-even avoids a forecast); comparing the mortgage payment with the rent alone (ignores that principal builds wealth).

## 2026-10-23 — Thirty-year national series from the BIS and the OECD, not spliced
- **What:** sources `bis` (WS_SPP, Portugal quarterly, 1988 on) and `oecd` (DSD_AN_HOUSE_PRICES, annual); interim tables `bis_spp` and `oecd_house_prices`; processed `national_annual_long` (1988 to 2025) with the BIS annual mean rebased to 2015, a real index from the project CPI, and the OECD rent, price-to-rent and price-to-income. Notebook `p1_03`.
- **Why no splice:** from 2009 the BIS index is INE's exactly (constant ratio over 69 quarters), so the BIS series already is the long INE series; before 2009 nothing overlaps, so a join cannot be tested. Re-chaining would only hide that, so the seam is marked on the chart and written in `data_quality.md`.
- **Why the project deflator:** the BIS real series equals the INE CPI deflation (ratio 1.000), the OECD's differs by up to 4%; one deflator everywhere (`harmonize.py`).
- **Scope:** national only. There is no municipal price history before 2016 and none was found; RQ1.1 stays partly answered. The INE census 1991 and 2001 and older population (supply-demand balance back to 1991) were offered and not chosen.
- **Rejected:** the Dallas Fed's extension to 1975 (spliced with building permits, not a price index), INE bank valuations (2011 on), reconstructing municipal prices from census values.

## 2026-10-23 — Regional outlook (RQ10.2): trends on rolling origins, stated relative to the median municipality
- **What:** `analysis/regional_forecast.py`, `p4_03`. Seven methods on log price-to-income (naive, national drift, national trend, own trend, region trend, coast x density class trend, empirical-Bayes shrunk trend), scored on rolling origins 2019 to 2023 at one and two years, same municipalities for all; selection rule fixed first (lowest municipal RMSE, ties within 1% to the simpler) picks the class trend. Intervals: empirical quantiles of errors, coverage checked leaving each origin out (79%).
- **Why not ETS/ARIMA per municipality:** nine points, noisy level (lag-1 growth autocorrelation -0.3 to -0.6): nothing to validate on. A municipality's own trend loses to no change; a trend shared across a group wins.
- **Relative, not absolute:** the common swing (2023 jump, 2025 price rise) is not predictable from municipal history and the simulator already carries the national path (+26% 2024 to 2026 against +8% from the municipal trend), so the outlook is the change relative to the median municipality, with intervals from relative errors. Ranking skill is scored with Spearman and top-fifth precision; the naive forecast has none by construction.
- **Smoothed level:** the trend methods forecast from the municipality's average level on a group slope, not from the last noisy value; the change is still measured from the last published value, so single municipalities show reversion (Armamar 2.4 to 4.8). Reported as a caveat; classes and regions are the reading unit.
- **Rejected:** a regression on drivers (phase 2 shows geography absorbs them), a regional multiplier inside the simulator (level not settled), forecasting rents (too few years and municipalities).

- **What:** `viz/dashboard.py` holds the indicator catalogue (label, unit, which direction is worse), the panel with the typology attached, the map values and the p2/p3 report tables; pages import from it and from nothing under `data/raw` or `data/interim`. The map uses a 400 m-simplified GeoJSON committed in `data/processed/municipalities.geojson` (`maps.export_web_geometry`), because the CAOP interim table is not in the repository and a cloned repo must run the app.

Changes use the blue-red ramp with the midpoint at zero, and red is always the direction that is worse for affordability (a fall in income or completions is red).
- **Explanatory page:** shows the committed phase 2 and 3 tables (variance ladder, segment changes, within-municipality coefficients, event study) and a scatter explorer of the 2016 to 2024 change in price-to-income against candidate explanations. The scatter is labelled a raw correlation across places; the adjusted estimates are the regressions tab.
- **Rejected:** a mapbox/MapLibre basemap (needs tile downloads), reading `data/interim` (not reproducible from a clone), recomputing regressions in the app (logic stays in `src/`, results in the tables).

## 2026-10-23 — Phase 4 simulator: stock-flow model pinned to the ETS forecast, parameters as ranges
- **What:** `simulation/{scenarios,supply,fiscal,engine}.py`, assumptions in `config/scenarios.yaml`. Annual, national, 2025 to 2035. Households = population (natural change + net migration) / persons per household (census trend); usable dwellings added = completions (net of demolitions) and levers; *balance pressure* = households formed beyond them, over households. Real price growth = flexibility x pressure + income elasticity x income growth - rate semi-elasticity x mortgage-rate change (two-year lag) + a fading other-demand drift + capitalisation of tax changes. Price-to-income and mortgage burden of the 2024 median municipality, rolled to 2025 with the real price index, are moved by the national price, income and rate paths. Direct effects (units, euros) and behavioural responses are separate code (`supply`/`fiscal` return counts, euros and shares of the price; only `engine` turns them into prices).
- **Pin to the forecast:** the starting drift of "other demand" is solved in closed form so that the baseline reproduces the ETS forecast for 2028Q2 (mean, with the 80% interval as the spread, one standard-normal draw per run); the fade (half-life 1, 2, 4 years by scenario) is an assumption and the largest source of difference between scenarios. Why: p4_01 validated only 8 quarters and a 20-quarter ETS extrapolates the boom to nonsense; this keeps the validated forecast as the start of every path and makes every behavioural parameter act only through deviations from it. Consequence: the long-run level barely depends on the behavioural parameters (they are recalibrated), only on the scenario inputs and the fade; they matter for the effect of a lever.
- **Scenario names** describe pressure on affordability ("optimistic" = favourable), not a judgement on migration. Inputs: net migration 40k / 80k / 140k (INE 2023-24: 156k, 144k), real income growth 2.5 / 1.5 / 0.5% (2015-24: 3.3%, 2021-24: 2.3%), mortgage rate in three years 2.5 / 3.25 / 4.5% (2024: 4.36%, 2025: 3.07%), completions in three years 50k / 38k / 27k (2025: 27.3k, licences 42k). All assumptions bracketing observed values.
- **Parameters:** each is a `Range` (low, mid, high) with a status: *literature* (price flexibility 1.0 to 2.0 from owner/renter demand elasticities -1.0 to -0.5, best estimates -0.6 to -0.7, Goodman and Kawai; rate semi-elasticity 3 to 5% per pp, ECB WP 2789 and Economic Bulletin 6/2022), *project* (usable share 0.69 to 0.95 and net additions per completion 0.89 from the census; IMI capitalisation bound from p3), *assumption* (income elasticity 0.5 to 1.0, additionality, reoccupation, crowd-out, unit costs). Where no estimate exists the range is a bound, the mid value is its centre and the report says so; no mid value is presented as an estimate (the p3 rule on behavioural parameters).
- **Uncertainty:** 1,000 joint triangular draws, common random numbers across scenarios and policies (so a policy effect is a paired difference); the drift is recalibrated per draw. The bands are parameter uncertainty; scenarios are conditional paths and are not given probabilities.
- **Policy levers (from 2027):** extra completions, vacant-home renovation, public housing (units a year), a change of the average IMI rate (pp), a change of the average IMT rate on dwellings (pp of price). IMI capitalisation = cut x (assessed value/price)/discount rate x share (the p3 benchmark, 0.19% per 0.01 pp at mid), run from 0 to 1 because p3 could not identify it. IMT: a buyer-paid tax lowers the price by its share if capitalised. The first-buyer exemption of the placeholder yaml is dropped (needs the first-buyer share and brackets, none ingested); IMT volume responses are not modelled.
- **Fiscal bases:** national totals typed in the yaml (IMI 1,630 million EUR, IMT 1,732.8 million EUR, 2024); IMI revenue loss proportional to the rate change over the simple mean municipal rate; IMT revenue loss = pp x the value of dwellings sold (panel sales x real price x 100 m²). Order-of-magnitude, not budget forecasts (data_quality.md).
- **Budget search (RQ10.5):** every split of an annual budget over the five levers in steps of 25%, at mid unit costs, ranked by median gain. The model is linear in each lever, so the best mix is a corner (all to the lever with the highest gain per euro); the unit-cost check shows the ranking depends on assumed costs.
- **Rejected:** a structural housing model with an estimated supply elasticity (none estimable from 70 quarters or from 9 annual municipal points; p2 associations fade with coast and density); pinning to a long ETS fan; per-municipality simulation (a national path moved proportionally is all the data support, regional detail is RQ10.2); using the p2 supply-affordability coefficients as price flexibility (adjusted associations, supply follows prices); a rent module (rents exist from 2020 only); an optimiser over lever sizes with diminishing returns (no evidence for the curvature).
- **Checked:** the calibrated baseline reproduces the forecast exactly (test), an empty policy changes nothing, each supply lever lowers the ratio and costs money in every draw, the IMI benchmark equals the p3 figure (0.19% per 0.01 pp), and the dashboard page runs under Streamlit's AppTest.

## 2026-10-23 — Phase 4 forecasting: ETS on the national quarterly series, two-year horizon
- **What:** `analysis/forecasting.py`: naive, seasonal naive, drift, ETS (six forms by AICc) and ARIMA(p,1,q) with or without drift (by AICc), rolling-origin validation (first window 36 quarters, refit at every origin, horizons 1 to 8), RMSE, MASE, bias and interval coverage. Targets: log real house price index and the ownership cost index (real price index x payment per 100,000 EUR, `features/affordability.py`). Selection rule fixed before looking: lowest mean RMSE over horizons on both series; ties to the simpler method. Result: ETS (RMSE 0.54 and 0.76 of the naive forecast), ARIMA second.
- **Why national, quarterly:** municipal series have 9 to 10 annual points, too short to validate; there is no quarterly national income, so the affordability indicator that can be forecast is the income-neutral cost of ownership, and the burden is formed in the simulator from an income scenario.
- **Horizon capped at the validated 8 quarters:** a 20-quarter ETS fan (tried first) extrapolates the undamped boom trend to a real price index of 178 in 2031 (80% range 91 to 348). Five to ten year paths are scenarios, with the two-year forecast as the starting point.
- **Known weakness:** every origin lies in the 2017 to 2026 boom, so a trend model is favoured and no turning point is tested; ETS intervals under-cover the cost index (95% interval holds 86%).
- **Rejected:** municipal ETS/ARIMA (too few points), ML models (the brief asks for a baseline win first and none is tried), a damped or longer-horizon fan without validation evidence.

## 2026-10-23 — Phase 3 estimation: stacked event study on isolated IMI cuts
- **What:** `analysis/causal.py`. Treated: isolated cuts (no other change from t-2 to t+1) in 2018-2023; controls: municipalities with no change from t-2 to t+2, compared within NUTS 3 x year (one stack per event year, unit and region-stack-year effects, errors clustered by municipality). Dose version (per 0.01 pp) as main, binary as check. Checks: cohort-by-cohort 2x2 with a municipality bootstrap, dose-response on all changes (with last year's growth and future cuts as placebo), nine sample variants. Benchmark: full capitalisation = cut x (assessed value / price) / discount rate, ranges in `config/scenarios.yaml`.
- **Why:** the stacked design uses only clean comparisons, so it is not biased by differing effects between event years; the cohort 2x2 is the check on that. Rates are chosen, so the pre-period coefficients (k = -2) decide whether the result may be called causal (rule fixed before estimating: p < 0.10 means adjusted association).
- **Controls must be quiet through t+post, not only through the isolation window (t+1):** a synthetic test showed that controls cutting in t+2 bias the k = +2 coefficient (0.0074 instead of 0.01).
- **Event-study coefficients are levels against k = -1**, so the two-year effect is the k = +2 coefficient, never a sum of coefficients; sums are used only for the dose-response (coefficients on changes).
- **Parish-link municipalities are those showing the link in any year from 2013:** the 2012 pages show it for 206 municipalities (another convention), which would drop 245 of 278.
- **Result:** inconclusive and not causal; see `reports/p3_policy.md`. Phase 4 receives a bound (0 to full capitalisation), not an estimate.
- **Rejected:** a two-way fixed-effects event study over all changes (biased with staggered timing and effects that differ by cohort), a rent outcome as a main result (2020 start, 5 treated municipalities), Callaway-Sant'Anna with covariates (the cohort 2x2 is the same comparison without the extra model).

## 2026-10-22 — IMI rate ingestion
- **What:** `sources/at.py` downloads one page per (tax year 2012-2025, district list) (308 pages, 0.8 s apart, research User-Agent) into `data/raw/at/imi_rates_<date>/` as received, with a manifest written last; parsing reads the snapshot only. Interim table `at_imi_rates` (308 municipalities x 14 years, no row dropped), checked by schema and consistency checks in `validate.py`.
- **Why:** the portal shows rates as stored today, so the dated snapshot is the record of what was seen; an interrupted download resumes (no manifest = incomplete) and a complete snapshot is reused unless `refresh`, so nobody else's server is hit 300 times a day. Each page's title year must equal the year asked for (the portal answers 200 for anything).
- **Island municipalities:** the portal numbers them by district list (19xx to 22xx), so an explicit 30-entry code map to INE codes (tested to cover every island municipality once) replaces name matching, which fails for 6 names (e.g. 'S. VICENTE').
- **Checked in `p3_01` (2026-10-23):** 2025 is complete (271 municipalities with a rate, 36 changes, in line with earlier years); the meaning of the 'Taxas por freguesia' link stays unverified and is handled as a sensitivity run (data_quality.md).

## 2026-10-22 — Phase 3 policy: municipal IMI rate changes
- **What:** phase 3 evaluates whether cuts in the municipal urban IMI rate are capitalised into sale prices (and, as a secondary outcome, rents), an event study on the changes in the rate each municipality sets every year. Source and design plan: `reports/p3_policy.md`; rates from the Portal das Finanças (`config/sources.yaml`, `at_imi_rates`).
- **Why this policy:** among five candidates (IMI rates, short-term-rental rules, IMT Jovem, golden-visa restrictions, rent cap / public housing) it is the only one with many treated municipalities and staggered timing, the rate data turned out to be collectable for 2012–2025, and a capitalisation estimate is the behavioural parameter the phase 4 IMI lever needs (RQ7.4).
- **Checked before choosing:** 2016–2025, 152 of the 270 municipalities with a complete rate series changed their rate at least once (509 changes, almost all cuts, median size 0.01 pp); on the mainland 303 changes fall in 2018–2023 with a complete window and 71 of them are isolated (no other change two years before or one after).
- **Limits known up front:** rates are set strategically (cuts follow local conditions), changes are small (a 0.01 pp cut is about 3% of the bill), the municipal price series starts in 2016, and the 113 never-changers are mostly at the 0.3% floor (98) so they cannot cut. An exploratory two-way fixed-effects fit (only its standard error was looked at) puts the standard error of the price response to a 0.01 pp cut at about 0.35 percentage points, several times the capitalisation one would expect, so the design can bound a pass-through, not confirm a small one.
- **Rejected:** short-term rental rules (adopters are the hottest markets, one-year national moratorium), IMT Jovem (everyone treated, no transaction data), golden visas (no municipal series, treated and comparison places already diverge), rent cap and public housing (no usable comparison, no data).

## 2026-10-22 — Phase 2 synthesis
- **What:** one cross-section of the mainland municipalities (outcome: log change of price-to-income 2016 to 2024) with blocks added in order (geography, demand, supply, tourism, second homes) and the cumulative R2 and adjusted R2 reported; the reverse order (drivers first) and a joint per-sd model with and without coast and density classes as checks.
- **Why:** the single-driver results of P2.02 to P2.06 overlap; a ladder shows how much each block accounts for beyond the others. Reporting both orders keeps the conclusion from depending on the order.
- **Rejected:** Shapley/dominance decompositions (the blocks are far from independent and the story is already clear from the two orders); presenting the per-sd coefficients as a ranking of causes.
- **Reading:** R2 is accompanying variation, not causal weight; with 271 municipalities and one change, place and driver cannot be separated, which is the argument for a design with a comparison group in phase 3.

## 2026-10-22 — Second homes (phase 2)
- **What:** the census share of secondary residences (2011, 2021) enters as a cross-section: same-district regressions of the 2016 to 2024 change and the 2024 level of price-to-income (with and without coast and density class, and in subsamples), and as an interaction (centred, per 10 points) with the P2.02 panel drivers under municipality + year and municipality + NUTS 3 × year effects. Mainland only.
- **Why:** only two census points exist, so a municipality fixed effect would absorb the share in the panel; an interaction is the way to ask whether the drivers act differently where second homes are common.
- **Rejected:** interpolating annual second-home counts between censuses (invented variation); using registered short-term rentals as the second-home measure (it is the touristic part only, and survivors); a causal reading of the negative cross-section sign (it reflects where second homes are: rural, low-demand places).

## 2026-10-22 — Supply against demand (phase 2)
- **What:** two periods with the same sign (positive = dwellings grew faster than households): the census decade 2011 to 2021 (observed: conventional dwellings and private households) and 2021 to 2023 (implied: new households = population growth / persons per household in 2021, supply = completions of 2022 and 2023). Groups are summed in counts and the ratios recomputed (never an average of municipal ratios). Cross-section regressions of the 2016 to 2024 log change in price-to-income on the drivers with district intercepts (mainland, HC3 errors), then with coast and density classes.
- **Why:** annual household counts do not exist, so the post-2021 pressure can only be implied from population; keeping the two periods apart shows which part is a count and which a proxy. A cross-section is used because dwellings and households are census-year observations.
- **Rejected:** treating the implied gap as a count (it ignores household-size change, demolitions, renovation and revisions of population estimates); a panel regression with interpolated annual households (invented variation); an instrument for construction (none available).
- **Caveat:** the implied gap is almost minus the population growth (correlation −0.99) because completions are small against it, so it adds little beyond population change; the 2016 to 2024 outcome overlaps both periods.

## 2026-10-22 — Municipality typology for group comparisons (phase 2)
- **What:** `data/external/muni_typology.csv` from `data/typology.py`. Coast: a CAOP boundary segment typed 'Limite e Linha de Costa' touches the municipality; 'estuary' if its only water boundary is a 'Limite na Água' (nine municipalities on the tidal Tejo and Sado, Lisboa among them); else inland. Density: residents per km² in 2021, urban from 500, rural below 100. Tourist: at least 25 registered short-term rentals per 1,000 dwellings (mainland; islands 'unknown'). Thresholds live in `config.py`.
- **Why:** these can be computed from sources already in the project, with rules that can be stated in one line. Segment comparisons use the 278 mainland municipalities (all 30 island municipalities are coastal and have no rental registry).
- **Rejected:** the INE TIPAU parish typology (not ingested; a density proxy is used and labelled as such); a hand-made list of coastal municipalities (not reproducible); treating Lisboa as inland because CAOP draws its river front as a water limit (it is kept in its own 'estuary' group); a tourism flag from overnight stays (no municipal source ingested yet).
- **Limits:** the tourist flag describes today's registry (survivors) and is applied to all years; the estuary group has 9 municipalities. The group differences do not depend on the borders (class thresholds varied in `p2_04`).

## 2026-10-22 — Spillover tests as fixed-effects regressions (phase 2)
- **What:** three tests with the p2_02 model and queen weights: Moran's I of the residuals; the neighbours' drivers (SLX, `spatial_lag` of each driver); the neighbours' log price-to-income last year, against next year (a placebo that cannot be a cause), with and without the municipality's own lag. Window 2017-2022. Municipality + year and municipality + NUTS 3 × year effects.
- **Why:** `spreg` (spatial-lag / spatial-error panel models) is not installed and installing software was not asked for; these are the space-time and SLX forms that a fixed-effects regression estimates directly, and the lead placebo is a falsification test a spatial-lag model would not give.
- **Reading:** NUTS 3 × year effects absorb spillovers that spread across a whole region, so the test asks whether neighbours matter beyond the regional pattern (conservative). Own-lag coefficients carry Nickell bias with 8 years.
- **Rejected:** a lagged dependent variable as the main specification (bias, and the municipality effect already carries persistence); Arellano-Bond (too few years for a stable GMM); a spatial Durbin model through a new package.

## 2026-10-22 — Foreign residents from AIMA (via INE) in the panel
- **What:** indicator 0010247 (foreign residents with a legal residence title, by NUTS 2013 municipality and nationality, 2014-2023) is fetched through `sources/aima.py`, cleaned into `aima_foreigners` (ten named nationalities, 'other', 'total'; ISO alpha-2 codes in lower case) and added to the panel as `foreign_residents_n` (total) and `foreign_residents_pct` (of the INE resident population). Regressions use the change in foreign residents and the change in the rest of the population, both over last year's population, so the two add up to the population's growth.
- **Why:** it is the only municipal immigration measure available (the INE population series carries no migration split at this level); the additive split lets the regression compare the two parts on one scale.
- **Rejected:** the AIMA migration report PDFs for 2024-2025 (not machine readable here); using levels or shares (they mix a place's size and history with its change; the fixed effects remove most of that anyway); nationality-specific drivers (kept in the interim table for later).
- **Caveat:** the series counts titles at year end and jumps 34% in 2023, possibly partly administrative; one specification stops in 2022 (data_quality.md).

## 2026-10-22 — Fixed-effects regressions of price-to-income (phase 2)
- **What:** outcome log price-to-income; drivers change in foreign residents and change in other population (see the AIMA entry above), completions of the previous three years per 1,000 census-2011 dwellings, share aged 65+, and (mainland) registered short-term rentals per 1,000 census-2011 dwellings. Municipality + year effects; harder variant municipality + NUTS 3 × year effects; errors clustered by municipality, by NUTS 3 region as a check. 2016 to 2023.
- **Why:** price-to-income is the affordability outcome. Income is left out of the drivers because it is inside the outcome, and because it varies little within a municipality (about 1% a year against 15% for prices) and is negatively correlated with price growth across municipalities (-0.31), so a price regression returns a negative income coefficient that reflects convergence, not an effect (shown in the notebook appendix). The 2011 dwelling stock is a fixed denominator so it does not move with construction. Completions are lagged (t-3 to t-1) so they cannot react to year-t prices. Results are reported per within-municipality standard deviation so drivers are comparable.
- **Rejected:** instruments for construction (no credible instrument available here, so effects stay adjusted associations); random effects (municipality effects are correlated with the drivers); a spatial-lag model in this step (residual Moran's I is flagged instead, the spatial model is the next step).
- **Limits kept visible:** residuals stay spatially correlated in some years; second homes are not in the panel (foreign residents are, see the AIMA entry); the rental stock is survivors only.

## 2026-10-21 — Spatial weights and tests (phase 2)
- **What:** queen contiguity, row-standardised, built separately for the mainland, the Azores and Madeira so no link crosses the sea; a municipality with no land neighbour (an island with one municipality) is linked to its 2 nearest in the same territory. Robustness: 6 nearest centroids. Global Moran's I, LISA and Gi* use 999 conditional permutations, two-sided, `alpha` 0.05, fixed seed, one process (`n_jobs=1`).
- **Why:** contiguity is the standard reading of "neighbouring municipality"; per-territory blocks avoid linking Corvo to Mainland by distance; k-nearest shows how much results depend on the weights (it matters for the within-region result: Moran's I 0.20 vs 0.08).
- **Rejected:** inverse-distance weights (dense, dominated by the mainland's size); a multiple-testing correction on LISA (kept as exploratory, stated on the map); the municipality mean as the only fix for regional effects (the NUTS 3 mean is used for the within-region check, FE regressions come next).
- **Reading:** Moran's I is a pattern, not proof of spillovers; Gi* and LISA flag the same municipalities (they are closely related), so their agreement is a consistency check.

## 2026-10-21 — Panels, deflator and affordability indicators
- **What:** `build_panel.py` writes `panel_municipality_year` (308 × 1995–2025) and `national_quarterly` (2005Q1+); `harmonize.annual_deflator` / `quarterly_deflator` / `to_real` deflate to 2024 euros; `features/affordability.py` holds PTI, RTI, mortgage burden and the supply gap, defined in `docs/methodology.md`.
- **Dwelling size:** INE prices and rents are per m², so a dwelling is the per-m² value times the census mean useful area of the municipality (interpolated between 2011 and 2021). It describes occupied homes and biases levels upwards, hence the fixed 100 m² benchmark variants. Rejected: one invented national size without a source.
- **Annual price = 4th-quarter value** of INE's last-12-months median (no averaging of overlapping windows).
- **Mortgage payment rate = agreed rate on new floating loans** (BPstat), not the APRC (1.1–1.8 pp higher since 2015, includes fees and insurance). LTV 80% and 30 years are assumptions in `config.py`; the effect of changing them is tabulated in methodology.md (2024 median burden 40–57%).
- **Supply gap** uses all conventional dwellings against private households between the 2011 and 2021 censuses, per 1,000 households at the start; annual household counts do not exist.
- **Short-term rentals** in the panel: islands are NaN, mainland municipalities without units are 0.
- **Census size filters**: the 2011 and 2021 dwelling and household tables exceed INE's row limit, so only the totals of the dimensions we do not split by are requested (`INE_FILTERED`).

## 2026-10-21 — Interim tables and validation
- **What:** `interim.py` builds one parquet per dataset in `data/interim/` (16 tables); `validate.py` checks them with pandera schemas (types, ranges, categories, unique keys, no unexpected columns), grid checks (308 municipalities in every period), consistency checks (totals = sum of parts, source publication rules) and cross-source checks (INE vs Eurostat HPI and CPI/HICP). `scripts/run_pipeline.py` runs download, build and validate.
- **Conventions:** municipal tables keep the full grid, with suppressed cells NaN (not dropped, not zero); nominal values, unit in the column suffix; index base in the column name (`_b2025`, `_b2015`); splices made at table-build time (prices 2018 -> 2022 method; income 2013 -> 2024 vintage; construction masks pre-creation years); rents are annual only (the semi-annual series is not built).
- **Thresholds come from the data:** a check is written for what the source guarantees, not tuned to pass. Examples: completions size split must never exceed the total and, from 2003, miss under 0.1% of dwellings (measured 0.008%); a published rent needs at least 33 new leases.
- **Rejected:** dropping suppressed rows (hides the grid and the coverage), one check per file inside the builders (validation stays separate and re-runnable).

## 2026-10-21 — Rates, Eurostat and RNAL ingestion
- **What:** one JSON-stat parser (`sources/jsonstat.py`) for Eurostat and BPstat; each BPstat series stored with its metadata and the dataset response filtered to it (dataset and domain are looked up from the series id, nothing hardcoded); Eurostat queries are parameter dicts in `sources.yaml` with a peer set (ES, IT, FR, DE, IE, EU27). RNAL is downloaded in pages and kept as a dated snapshot.

## 2026-10-21 — Municipality key and crosswalk
- **What:** the 308 CAOP 2024 municipalities (4-digit `dtmn`) are the master list; `muni_code` is the key everywhere. NUTS III/II codes per vintage in `data/external/muni_crosswalk.csv` are read from the 7-char INE `geocod` prefix of one NUTS 2013 series (0009490) and one NUTS 2024 series (0012600). INE municipality rows are kept only if their code is in the crosswalk (`keep_municipalities`); the rest is reported by `unmatched_codes`.
- **Why:** INE changes NUTS III codes between vintages (176 of 308 municipalities differ 2013 vs 2024) but the 4-digit municipality code is stable, so the prefix must never be part of a key. Deriving the codes from INE avoids typing a 308-row table by hand.
- **Islands:** CAOP geometries are reprojected to EPSG:3763 for one common CRS; this distorts island geometry, so areas come from CAOP `area_ha`, not from the geometry.
- **1990s (checked on the 1995 licensing series):** INE returns all 308 current municipalities for every year from 1995 with no extra or retired codes, but does not back-cast: Vizela and Trofa are exact zeros until 1998, Odivelas until 1999 (parents Guimarães, Santo Tirso/Maia, Loures still hold the territory). Crosswalk column `valid_from_year` (first year with data, from the first non-zero licensing year; to be confirmed against the creation laws) and `harmonize.mask_pre_creation` turn those zeros into NaN.
- **Splices:** price series — 2018 method until 3Q2019, 2022 method from 4Q2019 (median overlap ratio 1.002); income — 2013 vintage 2015–2017, 2024 vintage from 2018 (identical on the overlap).
